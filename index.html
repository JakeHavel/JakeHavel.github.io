I'm Jake, a Computer Science student at Stony Brook University, where I expect to complete my bachelor's degree in December 2026. My interest in technology begins with a simple question: what makes it possible for people and information to connect so quickly and reliably? The answer reaches far beyond a single device. It involves networks, operating systems, security decisions, and the careful work of the people who maintain them. Understanding those layers is what keeps me curious.

At Stony Brook, I have built a foundation through courses in computer security, web security, systems administration, systems fundamentals, and technical communications. I have also been named to the Dean's List for Fall 2023 and Spring 2025. These experiences have helped me connect classroom concepts to the practical questions I want to keep exploring: how systems are configured, how access is managed, and how infrastructure can be made more resilient.

That interest has taken shape in hands-on settings. During an IT Infrastructure Externship with Elite Computer Systems, I supported Windows and VMware environments, diagnosed networking issues, configured Fortinet firewalls, and managed Active Directory accounts. In an academic project, I configured a Linux virtual machine and deployed a LAMP-based WordPress server, adding security controls and automated backups. Each experience has shown me a different part of the same challenge: useful technology depends on thoughtful setup, clear processes, and ongoing care.

I also work as a cashier at Costco, where I have learned to stay attentive and communicate clearly in a busy, customer-facing environment. During a company fundraising campaign, I helped raise $2,589 for Cohen's Children's Hospital and ranked among the top three part-time cashiers for donation collections. That result matters to me because it connects everyday work with a tangible community benefit.

This portfolio brings those parts of my experience together. Visitors can explore my résumé, academic and work highlights, interests in networking and security, and professional profiles. My goal is to keep learning how dependable systems are built and maintained, while developing the technical judgment and communication skills to contribute meaningfully to that work.
